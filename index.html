What is Symphony Rendering?

We introduce Symphony Rendering, a conditional orchestration system that maps a melody—either from MIDI or a piano reduction—into full symphonic audio in the style of a target composer. Our Flow‑Matching Transformer conditions on time‑aligned transcription (piano‑roll) features and a global composer label, enabling faithful melodic realization with controllable orchestral style.

Trained on a curated 12‑composer YouTube corpus (~62 hours), our model supports two inference modes: MIDI→Symphony and Audio→Symphony (style transfer from piano reductions).

Abstract

We propose a symphony rendering system that converts a melody—either MIDI or a piano solo—into full symphonic audio in the style of a target composer. Our approach uses a flow‑matching Transformer conditioned on time‑aligned transcription features and a global composer label, enabling faithful realization of melodic content while controlling orchestral style. Trained on a curated 12‑composer YouTube corpus (~62 hours), the system supports two inference modes: MIDI‑to‑symphony rendering and audio‑to‑symphony style transfer from piano reductions (e.g., Mahler’s Symphony No. 10). We release resources including the list of source recordings with YouTube URLs, training code, pretrained checkpoints, and the transcription model, together with audio demos on our project page.

Method (Key Ideas)

  1. Transcription Model. A multi‑instrument AMT extracts a time‑aligned piano‑roll \(\hat r\in[0,1]^{T\times K}\) from orchestral audio. This enables training on audio‑only data by pairing \(\hat r\) with the reference waveform.
  2. VAE Latent Space. A pretrained audio VAE encodes the waveform into latents \(z\in\mathbb{R}^{T'\times D}\). Generation is performed in latent space and decoded to waveform at 48 kHz.
  3. Flow Matching. We learn a conditional vector field \(v_\theta(z,t,r',c')\) along a straight displacement path \[ z_t = (1-t)z_0 + t z_1, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}=\mathbb{E}\,\|v_\theta(z_t,t,r',c')-(z_1-z_0)\|^2. \] The conditioning tokens include projected piano‑roll features \(r'\), a composer embedding \(c'\), and a timestep embedding.
  4. Sampling (ODE Integration). Starting from \(z_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), we integrate the learned probability‑flow ODE \(\tfrac{dz_t}{dt}=v_\theta(z_t,t,r',c')\) to obtain \(z_1\), then decode to audio.

Symphony Dataset

We curate a YouTube‑sourced orchestral corpus covering 12 composers (Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Brahms, Haydn, Shostakovich, Dvořák, Schubert, Rachmaninoff, Prokofiev, Tchaikovsky, Mahler), totaling 216 recordings (~62 h). We release the full list of source URLs and preprocessing scripts.

Model & Training

  • 12‑block DiT (~150M params), RMSNorm, RoPE, AdaLN conditioning.
  • Audio VAE latent frame‑rate: ~25 Hz; AMT piano‑roll: 100 Hz.
  • 30 s clips; AdamW (1e‑4); warmup 1k steps; 4×RTX 4090.
